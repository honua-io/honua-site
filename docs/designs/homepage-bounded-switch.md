## Problem Statement

The founder asked for a review of honua.io for performance and for how well it communicates the value proposition. Performance is measured and fine. The value proposition is the problem: the redesigned homepage tells a platform story (headline, protocol inventory, diagram) to a buyer who has no customer story to trust and no behavioral evidence behind the pitch. The site's job for the next 30 days is narrower than "explain the platform": get a county GIS manager to reply to one of five outreach messages and book a scoped pilot.

Measured homepage performance (local server, uncompressed): 8 requests, about 185 KB, of which the two self-hosted fonts are 130 KB; no images; cumulative layout shift 0; first and largest contentful paint about 620 ms. No performance work is recommended.

## Demand Evidence

None that is behavioral. The founder chose "No behavioral evidence yet; the five launch outreach messages are the first real test." Nobody can be named who paid, booked a call, or ran the quickstart unprompted. Every copy decision below is therefore a hypothesis the outreach has to falsify.

## Status Quo

In the founder's words: "its all of it the price in license the complexity and cost of ops. the lockin that allow salespeople to bend you over. the shitty architecture they keep pushing of vms. but then the k8 esri is crazy expensive. the lack of serverless."

Landscape (generic category search, 2026-10-31): alternatives pitch "open and cheaper"; public-sector buyers discount "free" because implementation, support and migration are the real costs. Small-city GIS staff at FOSS4G 2026 cite upgrade pressure and retired products breaking live maps. County board records justify staying on the incumbent on price: the enterprise agreement beats per-seat licensing once user counts grow, and reverting costs more. No GIS manager was found complaining publicly about lock-in; the complaint lives inside board cost justifications. The conclusion that shaped the premises: the board already believes staying is the cheap option, so a cheaper pitch loses on arrival; the lever is removing the switching cost and the ops staffing requirement.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- The reader: the county GIS manager or coordinator. Owns the renewal, carries the one or two admins, gets blamed when the map is down. That they can sign a pilot from $10,000 without a procurement round is an assumption to test, not a fact. The developer who verifies is the second reader and gets the second door.
- The wedge the page sells: a bounded migration pilot on PILOT_OFFER.md's terms, 4 to 6 weeks, from $10,000, scoped in a 30-minute call, against a defined set of services and clients. Not a platform decision.

## Constraints

- No customer story, no numbers, no rates may be invented; every product claim links to evidence (capability registry, demos, claims ledger). Carve-out: the pilot's duration, price floor and boundary are a founder commercial decision, not a product claim. Their source is honua-sales docs/PILOT_OFFER.md (fixed-scope pilot, 4 to 6 weeks, $10k simple, $17.5k standard, $25k complex, scoped in a 30-minute assessment; the file is still headed DRAFT) and the 2026-10-27 launch decision in the gstack decision log (pilots from $10k). By approving this document on 2026-10-31 the founder confirmed the three public figures (4 to 6 weeks, from $10,000, a 30-minute scoping call) as the launch terms. On the site the pilot sub-line resolves to #contact, and the reply carries deliverables until a pilot page exists.
- Lead-capture contract: CTA attributes and hidden lead fields are validated; analytics never reads PII.
- Self-hosted fonts only; prepared head via the marketing-pages script.
- The headline "The AI-native geospatial platform." stays (founder decision this session).
- No Esri product names on the homepage; the incumbent is described by role.
- The install path is an evaluation build; the site already discloses there is no supported public 2026.1 release.

## Premises

1. The first reader is the county GIS manager; the developer gets the second door, not the headline.
2. Revised by the founder: the headline stays. The switching-cost removal (clients keep working, applications port by codemod) is stated in the buyer's words in the promise line, lede and datasheet.
