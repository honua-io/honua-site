# Honua 2026.1 support policy

Revision: **2026.1-r2**. Status: **published** for 2026.1 staffed-manual support.
Policy owner: Mike McDougall, <mike@honua.io>.
This revision publishes the severity, coverage, contact, response, mitigation,
update, security, incident, maintenance, and end-of-life terms reviewed as
2026.1-r1. Those terms are unchanged. r2 drops links into private repositories
so the same text can be given to customers.

## Scope and operating model

This policy covers the 2026.1 platform's supported production workflows and paid
pilot support. The 2026.1 decision selects **staffed-manual** support: the
honua-support application is **Preview**, and email plus a private manual case
record are the required support path. Using the application is optional.
This policy does not create a support marketplace, customer portal, telephone
hotline, public status page, automatic paging service, or automated support
product. Honua does not operate a production multi-tenant SaaS service for this
release.

The GA scope includes the whole geoprocessing catalog and COG, Zarr, GeoParquet,
and PMTiles, within the qualified operating envelope of the signed release lock.
Multi-tenancy is Preview/trial only, with no production deployment or customer
production data. Customer alerting and offline sync are Preview. Preview feature
functionality receives best-effort support without an availability or performance
SLA. Security, tenant isolation, data integrity, and defects affecting a GA
workflow retain their normal severity regardless of feature label or edition.
Community users can report defects and security issues; paid response objectives
apply to enrolled pilot customers. Security intake is available to everyone.

## 1. Severity definitions

Use the highest applicable impact. Support confirms severity with the customer at
first response and records any change, evidence, reason, and notification in the
case. A disputed downgrade goes to the policy owner; retain the higher severity
while the dispute is reviewed. Severity is based on impact, not account size.

| Severity / API alias | Objective impact | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sev1 Critical / `critical`, `p1`, `sev1` | A supported production service or essential workflow is unavailable with no safe workaround; confirmed or credibly suspected ongoing unauthorized access, cross-tenant disclosure, data loss, corruption, or silent wrong results. | All production queries fail; writes disappear after restart; a query returns another tenant's data; GP output silently changes coordinates or nodata into valid values. |
| Sev2 High / `high`, `p2`, `sev2` | A supported production workflow is materially impaired, with a safe temporary workaround or limited impact; significant security exposure without evidence of active exploitation or ongoing integrity loss. | Publishing fails but existing layers remain usable and a previous supported configuration works; severe query slowdown on a qualified workload. |
| Sev3 Medium / `medium`, `p3`, `sev3` | A limited defect with a safe workaround and no material production outage, security exposure, or integrity loss. | One documented client option fails in staging while a supported alternative works. |
| Sev4 Low / `low`, `p4`, `sev4` | Information, cosmetic, documentation, or enhancement request with no current operational impact. | Clarification of an install guide; a label typo; a request for a new feature. |

These Sev1–Sev4 names are customer incident severities. They are distinct from
engineering backlog priorities P0/P1. Suspected security reports are handled
privately while impact is assessed; lack of a complete reproduction does not
delay acknowledgement or precautionary containment.

## 2. Coverage and clocks

Coverage is **Monday–Friday, 09:00–17:00 Pacific/Honolulu (HST, UTC−10,
no daylight-saving change)**. Company holidays are January 1, July 4, and
December 25; a Saturday holiday is observed on the preceding Friday and a Sunday
holiday on the following Monday. These are the only closures in this policy.
Any additional closure requires advance customer notice and equivalent named
cover; it cannot silently reduce an existing response window.

One business hour is 60 minutes inside coverage; one business day is eight such
hours. Clocks start at receipt by any supported route, carry remaining time into
the next coverage window, and exclude closed hours. "By next business day" means
no later than 17:00 on the next covered date after receipt. For after-hours
receipt, the next covered date is that next business day. Calendar-day periods
run continuously, including weekends and holidays.

For example, a Critical report received Friday at 16:00 HST is due Monday at
12:00 HST if neither day is a holiday. If Monday is an observed holiday, it is
due Tuesday at 12:00 HST. A report received Saturday starts its four-hour clock
Monday at 09:00 and is due at 13:00 HST, absent a holiday.

Email accepts reports at any time; **24×7 response is not offered**. After-hours
reports enter the next coverage window unless a separately acknowledged staffed
change window applies. Customers should use their own incident response and
documented containment/rollback procedures immediately when needed.
The acknowledged coverage and backup roster, route tests, and handoff rehearsal
are maintained with the staffed-manual operating roster. This policy does not
attest that those operational receipts already exist.

## 3. Contact and fallback routes

| Purpose | Route | Fallback |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Paid pilot support and incident intake | Email **mike@honua.io**, subject `Honua support: <severity> <short impact>`; reply to the same thread after a case ID is assigned. | Email **info@honua.io** with the original receipt time and case ID, asking for the support owner. Use this if delivery fails or the first-response/update deadline passes. |
| Confidential vulnerability or security incident | Email **security@honua.io**, subject `Confidential Honua security report`. | Email **mike@honua.io** with a confidential handoff request and safe contact details; request a secure exchange before sending sensitive evidence. |
| Public, non-confidential product defect | [Honua Server issue intake](https://github.com/honua-io/honua-server/issues/new/choose). | Use the support email route if GitHub is unavailable or the report needs privacy. |

Public issues are not the paid support queue. A customer should email the public
issue URL to support to start paid case handling. No customer portal, telephone
hotline, public status page, automatic paging service, support marketplace, or
automated support product is promised by this revision. A call may be arranged
in the case thread; any customer-specific phone
or alternate incident route must be written into the acknowledged pilot handoff.
If both email routes fail, retain delivery evidence and use the alternate contact
from that handoff; a public issue may request contact using non-sensitive details
only. Do not post a confidential report publicly as a fallback.

Include customer contact, requested severity, UTC incident/receipt times, impact,
affected environment, platform version and lock digest if available, exact
component/image identity, reproduction steps, and sanitized diagnostics. Missing
identity or diagnostics does not delay first response. Do not email tokens,
credentials, customer datasets, or unredacted logs; request a private transfer.
Support assigns one case ID and a named human owner, retaining receipt times and
cross-references when fallback routes create duplicate threads.

## 4–6. Response, mitigation, and update objectives

| Severity | First human response | Mitigation target from receipt | Customer update cadence while open |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sev1 Critical | Within 4 business hours | Within 1 business day | At each material change and at least every 1 business day |
| Sev2 High | By next business day | Within 3 business days | At each material change and by the end of each subsequent business day |
| Sev3 Medium | Within 2 business days | Within 10 business days | At each material change and at least every 5 business days |
| Sev4 Low | Within 2 business days | Within 20 business days | At each material change and at least every 10 business days |

A first response is a human acknowledgement with case ID, confirmed impact,
owner, next action, and next update deadline. An automatic email, API response,
or machine diagnosis does not satisfy it. First-response clocks do not pause
while awaiting customer information or engineering assignment. The update clock
starts at first response and restarts when a substantive update is sent.

Mitigation means a communicated, validated safe workaround, containment,
rollback, or patch that reduces the reported impact. For information requests,
it means an actionable answer; for enhancements, an explicit disposition and
available alternative, without promising implementation. These are objectives,
not guaranteed fix deadlines. Support records what remains unmitigated, rather
than treating a plan alone as successful mitigation. If customer authorization
or access prevents validation, record the dependency and elapsed time; do not
silently reset the clock or mark the target met.

Permanent resolution has no universal time target. At triage, state the planned
fix or investigation milestone, owner, and next review date; revise these in each
update as evidence changes. Before a target is missed, escalate to the policy
owner and tell the customer the reason, current containment, revised estimate,
and next update time. Updates continue even when there is no technical progress
or the case is waiting on a customer/vendor. After mitigation, severity may be
changed only through the recorded severity process above.

The Preview API's five-minute and other elapsed-time diagnosis/validation
escalation windows are internal reminders, not these customer business-hour
objectives. Its SLA snapshots are a partial paid-pilot summary. The manual owner
tracks receipt, response, mitigation, updates, and closure against this policy;
application automation is not relied on to enforce the coverage calendar.

## 7. Confidential security handling

The [organization security policy](https://github.com/honua-io/.github/blob/main/SECURITY.md)
governs research scope and coordinated disclosure. General researcher intake
targets are acknowledgement within 3 business days, initial assessment within
10 business days, and updates at least every 10 business days. A paid customer
security incident also follows the impact-based table above; apply the earlier
applicable deadline. A component's more demanding published security target is
retained. Security investigations do not wait for public issue creation.

The recipient restricts access to the reporter, assigned security responders,
and authorized affected customer contacts. Keep exploit details and private
evidence out of public issues, general incident updates, and LLM triage. Request
the minimum sanitized reproduction and arrange a secure transfer if needed.
The policy owner assigns a security responder and an incident commander for
active exposure. Preserve evidence, contain exposure with customer approval,
identify affected versions, and coordinate advisory timing and mitigation with
the reporter. Public advisories identify affected/fixed artifacts and safe
recovery steps without revealing customer data or unremediated exploit details.

## 8. Incident declaration, escalation, communication, and closure

1. **Declare:** the receiving human declares an incident for every Sev1 and for
   suspected security, tenant-isolation, or data-loss/corruption events. Do not
   wait for root cause. Record a private incident ID linked to the case, impact,
   start/detection times, affected locks/artifacts, and the next update deadline.
2. **Assign and escalate:** the staffed support owner appoints a named incident
   commander, engineering responder, and customer communication owner from the
   acknowledged roster. Mike McDougall is the accountable escalation owner.
   An unavailable responder or approaching missed target triggers the roster's
   backup and escalation to the policy owner. A handoff is complete only when
   the replacement acknowledges ownership and the next update time in the case.
3. **Contain:** preserve diagnostics and backups; propose the least disruptive
   safe workaround or rollback. Read-only investigation is the first step.
   Production writes, scoped credentials, and break-glass require explicit,
   time-bounded, ticket-scoped customer approval with rollback intent. No standing
   credentials or unmanaged production changes are part of this support offer.
4. **Communicate:** use the private case thread for impact, scope, containment,
   actions, known unknowns, owner, and next update time at the table's cadence.
   Notify each affected customer's authorized contacts separately. A sanitized
   release advisory can supplement those notices for a widespread defect; it
   never replaces direct customer updates or exposes other customers' details.
5. **Fix and validate:** record affected/fixed platform lines, exact lock and
   artifact digests, reproduction and rollout receipts, and isolated backport/
   forward-port status. Reproduce the defect, retain a failing-before regression,
   qualify the candidate patch, and require a human fixed-version/patch-promotion
   approver distinct from the implementer. A merged PR is not an installed fix.
6. **Close:** verify the customer's installed identity, demonstrate the original
   reproduction now passes, and record either customer confirmation or 24 hours
   of stable operation. Complete required forward-ports through supported lines
   and trunk. A temporary mitigation may end the active incident with customer
   agreement, but the unresolved defect retains a linked open case. Do not mark
   a case resolved merely because the customer stops replying; record any
   agreed administrative closure separately and reopen on recurrence.
7. **Learn:** for Sev1, security, data loss/corruption, tenant isolation, or
   repeated release failures, share a sanitized postmortem within 5 business
   days of incident closure, with timeline, causes, recovery evidence, and
   corrective actions carrying owners and dates. If investigation is incomplete,
   deliver an interim report by that deadline and give the next report date.

## 9. Maintenance, supported versions, and end of life

For the first 90 calendar days after GA, review ready fixes on a weekly patch
train; publish only when qualification passes. Out-of-band patches address
outage, tenant isolation, data loss/corruption, high-impact security, or a severe
issue without a safe workaround. Availability of a patch does not authorize an
automatic change to a customer deployment.

Give affected customers **at least 5 business days' notice** of planned
Honua-assisted maintenance through their support thread. State the local and UTC
window, expected interruption, exact from/to locks, backup prerequisites,
validation, rollback or restore boundary, owner, and cancellation route. Obtain
customer approval for the window and production changes. For urgent security or
incident containment, notify as soon as the need is known, explain why normal
notice cannot be met, and retain the same explicit authorization. Send completion,
failure/rollback, or cancellation notice by the end of the agreed window; an
overrun becomes an incident with continued updates.

Initially **2026.1 is the sole supported maintenance line** after its GA.
Support diagnoses the reported released patch; fixes ship on the latest qualified
patch in that line, and customers may need to update to receive them. Supported
component combinations and upgrade edges come from the signed platform lock and
compatibility ledger, never a guessed version range or the Preview application's
embedded compatibility table. Patch releases must not introduce a breaking API,
contract-major change, destructive migration, or Preview graduation. Distinguish
application rollback from database rollback: a forward-only migration requires
the documented compatible forward fix or tested backup restore, not an assumed
reverse migration. Security fixes also reach trunk.

The 2026.1 maintenance period is **at least 12 calendar months from its actual GA
publication date**. The GA release notes must announce that date and the earliest
maintenance end date. Ending support additionally requires both **at least 90
calendar days' notice** and **one full subsequent platform release cycle of
notice**: from the start of that next line's announced development cycle through
its GA, with an available, documented upgrade path. The effective EOL is the
latest of those boundaries. If the next cycle or migration slips, support extends
until they are met. Before 2026.2, publish whether the long-term policy becomes
current-plus-previous; any overlap needed to honor 2026.1's announced minimum
remains supported even under an otherwise current-line-only policy.

At EOL, routine diagnosis and new maintenance patches end unless a written
extension applies; security reports are still accepted and assessed, and an
upgrade may be required for remediation. Notify known customers directly and
publish the final supported lock, migration steps, dates, and status in release
notes. Retain final artifacts, signatures, advisories, and upgrade documentation
after active maintenance ends. Never overwrite or delete a bad release to hide
it: mark its lock withdrawn, notify affected customers, and publish the exact
prior or corrective lock and safe recovery instructions.

## Revision and publication control

Revision 2026.1-r2 is the published customer policy. Customers receive this
text. An identical copy is published where pilot customers can read it without
access to a private repository. The publication record names the immutable
source commit and SHA-256 of this file.

A later change to these terms is a new revision. Notify affected customers at
least 30 calendar days before a reduction in terms, while honoring existing
agreements and lifecycle minimums.

The signed 2026.1 release lock's evidence must identify this revision and its
digest before that lock can be said to use it. Until that lock exists and is
checked, this publication does not claim release-lock binding.
